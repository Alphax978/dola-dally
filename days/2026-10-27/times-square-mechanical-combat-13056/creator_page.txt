PRIMARY VISUAL RULE — SPARK-FREE MECHANICAL COMBAT Do not generate flying sparks or glowing particle trails anywhere in the sequence. Show weapon contact through physical recoil, brief camera vibration, metallic sound, and a small cool-white daylight reflection confined to the contacting metal surfaces. This reflection does not scatter, spray, or leave a trail. All flying debris must look like solid manufactured material: silver metal fragments, dark steel pieces, white ceramic chips, recognizable gears, brackets, and electronic components. Fragments have hard edges, visible thickness, rigid rotation, and gravity-driven trajectories. Avoid clouds of tiny luminous specks. Keep destruction effects neutral in color. No red, orange, yellow, pink, or brown particle effects. No liquid-like jets, splashes, droplets, dripping, or wet textures. Dust is pale gray, dry, thin, and slowly settling. SETTING Times Square, New York City, in bright midday daylight. Giant LED billboards, tall commercial buildings, glass storefronts, projecting signs, maintenance platforms, streetlights, and broad intersecting streets. Blue sky, strong natural sunlight, crisp shadows, and neutral daylight color grading. The combat area is deserted. The fighters repeatedly move between street level and several stories above the intersection. Use walls, columns, billboard frameworks, and elevated platforms as physical rebound surfaces. Keep the streets visible below during aerial exchanges. Maintain consistent geography and accumulating damage. Broken buildings, fallen signs, glass, craters, and rubble remain damaged throughout the sequence. REFERENCE ASSIGNMENT REFERENCE IMAGE 1 = A, THE CYBERNETIC ONI SAMURAI. Preserve the reference silhouette and appearance: tall black horns, ivory-colored armored helmet, red electronic eye details, mechanical respirator, purple and acid-yellow armor, decorative chest plates, exposed machinery, hoses, heavy boots, and mechanical backpack. The helmet and chest ornamentation are manufactured armor. A carries two katana matching the reference. A moves through explosive jumps, wall rebounds, rapid pivots, ballistic momentum, and physical combat. A never teleports or transforms into mist. REFERENCE IMAGE 2 = B, THE MECHANICAL REAPER. Preserve the exact reference silhouette, proportions, color arrangement, markings, hanging fabric, chains, and enormous curved scythe with its suspended vessel. B is an entirely mechanical construct. The deer-skull-shaped face is an ivory-colored ceramic mask mounted over a metal head assembly. The branching antlers are manufactured metal structures with ceramic-coated surfaces. The rib-shaped chest decoration is artificial armor. The interior contains gears, motors, circuit boards, brackets, cables, and a central metal chassis. The hanging costume fabric is synthetic. Red reference details are rigid painted surfaces or small electronic indicator lights. Keep the reference appearance while making the construction unmistakably artificial. B remains visibly taller than A. Only B can transform into dense black mist and teleport. Reference colors remain attached to intact armor and equipment. During the final destruction, emphasize neutral metal interiors and white ceramic fracture surfaces. Do not turn painted colors or electronic lights into colored particle effects. Use the references only for character design. Do not display reference-sheet backgrounds, annotations, detail panels, borders, or duplicate character views. Do not exchange character identities, weapons, colors, or abilities. BLACK-MIST TELEPORTATION B's mechanical body and equipment disappear and reassemble together. Only one physical B exists at a time. The antlers, scythe, chains, and accessories always return with B. Each transition is extremely fast but readable: solid machine → localized black mist → relocation → mechanical reassembly. The mist remains monochrome black and charcoal gray, without colored droplets or glowing particles. B must become physically solid to attack. A eventually anticipates this reassembly moment. After the final successful cut, B remains solid and breaks apart mechanically. ACTION AND CAMERA RHYTHM Begin at exactly 0 seconds with A already being thrown violently backward and upward. Start directly inside the action. Make most of the battle airborne. Include actual midair sword-and-scythe exchanges, defensive rotations, rapid counters, knees, and kicks. Maintain strong momentum, powerful arcs, and fast descents. A redirects movement through contact with structures, weapons, and the opponent. B attacks through teleportation from above, below, behind, and across A's flight path. Use motivated cuts around 2, 4, 7, 10, and 13 seconds. The one-second intervals describe choreography beats, not mandatory cuts. Continue each shot between those transitions. Keep silhouettes, weapon contacts, and travel directions readable.
