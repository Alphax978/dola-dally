# Morning task (read by the Claude morning routine)

Every morning: pick TWO watermark-free videos from the video prompt galleries of YouMind, ReelCine and Domer (Seedance 2.0, 2.5 and other models), study each frame by frame, write ONE precise prompt per video that recreates it, save them for the day's posts, and report. Two videos per day. Nobody reviews the prompt before it is used, so it must be precise, complete and work on the first try.

CONTEXT: The user runs the Mira AI pages (X, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok). There is always exactly ONE prompt per video, however long the video is: no parts, no options. GitHub posts each ORIGINAL video with our prompt (on X as the reply): video 1 at 4:30 PM and video 2 at 9:30 PM Nepal time.

STEP 0 — Check who writes today. Read writer.txt in <repo> (after the git fetch/checkout below). If its setting line says gemini, Gemini writes the prompts on GitHub instead of you: end with a final reply "Writer is set to Gemini (writer.txt); nothing to do today." and do nothing else. Also, if prompts/x/<today's Nepal date>-1.json or -2.json already exists on origin/main (the Gemini backup already wrote today's prompts), don't write or overwrite anything: end with a final reply saying "Today's prompts were already written by the Gemini backup." Check this again right before STEP 4's commit.

STEP 1 — Get today's frames. A GitHub Action in the user's PUBLIC repo Alphax978/dola-dally (.github/workflows/prepare.yml, script prepare_frames.py) picks up to 4 random unused, watermark-free candidates (any length) from YouMind, ReelCine or Domer (sources rotate by day) and saves, for each, timestamped frame grids (frames_N.jpg: 30 frames per image, each stamped with its time; one frame every 0.5 s for short clips, sparser for long ones), bottom-of-frame text strips (text_N.jpg, for subtitles/on-screen text), creator_page.txt (the YouMind page text with the creator's prompt) and meta.json (title, YouMind url, x_post, duration, width, height, cuts = scene-cut times in seconds), listed in days/<YYYY-MM-DD>/index.json (Nepal date). It is scheduled for ~5:00 AM Nepal time, but GitHub sometimes skips or delays scheduled runs, so you start it yourself when needed.

STEP 2 — Study each of the two videos (do STEP 2 and STEP 3 fully for each one).
- Read meta.json, then look at EVERY frames_N.jpg and text_N.jpg image with the Read tool. Use the cut times to find each shot's start and end. For each shot note: framing (wide/medium/close-up/POV/selfie), camera movement, who is in it and what they do, setting, light, and any on-screen text or subtitles (the text strips show spoken lines when the video has subtitles). Note the aspect ratio from width/height.
- Also read creator_page.txt in the same folder: GitHub saved the source page's text there (YouMind, ReelCine or Domer; this environment can't open those sites), and it contains the creator's original prompt. Use it for intent, dialogue and sound that frames can't show. If that file is missing, work from the frames alone without mentioning it. Where the frames and the creator's prompt disagree, follow the frames.

STEP 3 — For each video, write ONE prompt that recreates it faithfully, for the video's full length. Keep the original's characters, setting, story, shots, timing, camera moves, look, sound and payoff as close to the original as possible: the goal is a replica, not a reinterpretation. Describe characters from what you actually see in the frames (age, skin, hair, face, clothing, signature details).
The only exception: if the original features copyrighted or franchise characters, or real people/celebrities, replace each of them with an original character who plays the same role in the same scene — new face, hair, build, costume, colors and emblems — and keep everything else identical. Don't carry over any signature look, costume, color scheme, emblem, catchphrase or name from those characters or people, and don't mention them in the prompt.
Leave out burned-in subtitles and on-screen text (AI video garbles text); keep the spoken lines as dialogue instead.
Format of the prompt:
- Header line: "VIDEO PROMPT — N seconds" (the original's real length), aspect ratio (same as the original), the look (realism/style, lens, color grade, light), the camera feel, and pacing.
- CHARACTERS block: concrete, fixed descriptions (age, skin, hair, face, clothing, one or two signature details visible in every shot). LOCATION block if relevant.
- Shot list with timecodes from 00:00 matching the real cuts (SHOT n (00:00.0-00:01.5) — title. what happens, framing, camera move, speed), using specific camera language (push-in, dolly, tracking, crane, whip pan, locked-off, low angle) and physical cause and effect.
- SOUND: per shot or as one block. Name the sounds; use an "upbeat instrumental beat" or similar instead of any real song; say "no music" when there should be none; keep spoken lines short and in double quotes, in the original's language.

FIDELITY RULES (video models drop or blur anything vague, so be concrete):
- One SHOT per real cut. The cut list in meta.json misses soft cuts and transitions, so find every change of shot in the frame grids yourself and give each its own SHOT line. Never lump several cuts into one long shot.
- Settings: name exactly what is visible: era and architecture (e.g. "modern glass skyscrapers and high-rise apartment blocks along a wide river with long road bridges" vs "traditional tiled-roof hanok houses"). Never write a mix like "modern city with traditional houses" unless both are clearly on screen, and then say which is in the foreground and which dominates.
- Hair, clothing and props: describe the state actually seen in each phase (e.g. "hair long and loose" vs "tied back"); don't guess.
- Signature elements: list every distinct object or effect the original shows (a water sphere, wings, a glowing magic circle, a vortex...), and in the shot where each appears write it as "MUST SHOW: ..." with its look, size and position in frame.
- Camera angle of each key moment exactly as seen: side view vs top-down, close-up vs wide, from behind vs from the front.
- Before saving, review your prompt against the frame grids one frame at a time: for each frame, check the prompt already describes what is on screen at that time. Fix anything missing, wrong or out of order, then save.

STEP 4 — Save to GitHub (the posting reads these files).
- In <repo> (on main, as set up in STEP 1): git pull --ff-only origin main, then write these files (Nepal date for <date>):
  - prompts/x/<date>-1.json (video 1) and prompts/x/<date>-2.json (video 2), written with python3 and json.dump(..., ensure_ascii=False, indent=1), never by hand-typing JSON. Fields:
      "date": "<YYYY-MM-DD>", "slot": 1 or 2, "slug": <that video's slug from index.json>,
      "caption": exactly TWO lines separated by a newline, at most 180 characters in total, no links, no hashtags, no @mentions. EACH LINE IS EXACTLY ONE SHORT SENTENCE: never two sentences in a line, no "..." trailing off, ideally under 10 words per line. Short and catchy beats descriptive. Line 1: the hook, one short punchy sentence about what makes the clip striking. Line 2: one short sentence that names the model from meta.json's "model" field in a natural way, e.g. "Seedance 2.5 nailed every frame of this." or "All made with Seedance 2.0, no camera." (vary it every day, never copy these examples). If meta.json has no "model", line 2 is a second punchy line without a model name. "Prompt: ⤵️" is added automatically below, so don't include it.
      "prompt": that video's full prompt,
      "prompt_short": the same prompt condensed to at most 270 characters (style, main character, the key action, mood). It is only used if X refuses the full prompt.
  - breakdowns/<date>-1.json and breakdowns/<date>-2.json — the TikTok "prompt breakdown" script for each video (TikTok gets a 1-minute+ version: hook, the original, then key moments replayed with the prompt words that made them, read by a voice). Write them with python3 json.dump(..., ensure_ascii=False, indent=1). BREAKDOWN SCRIPT FORMAT:
      {"hook": {"from": <second where the most striking 3-5 s moment starts>, "text_top": <the caption's line 1, max 45 characters>, "text_bottom": "Wait for the ending..." (always exactly this), "voice": <exactly the caption's line 1, word for word>},
       "intro_voice": "Here's the original.",
       "shots": [ {"n": <shot number from your prompt> OR "label": <for clips without real cuts: "The look", "The trigger", "The face", "The camera", "The secret"...>,
                   "from": <start second>, "to": <end second> (the matching moment in the ORIGINAL clip, from your shot timecodes),
                   "excerpt": <that shot's part of YOUR prompt, condensed to at most 200 characters, still in prompt language; write names in normal case, not capitals>,
                   "highlight": [2-4 exact phrases copied from the excerpt: the camera move, framing, light or the signature detail],
                   "voice": <one or two spoken sentences, max 25 words, explaining what the words make happen, e.g. "Shot two. A close-up with a very slow push-in. She raises her eyes into the lens.">} ],
       "outro_voice": "The full prompt is in the caption. Follow Mira AI for a new one every day."}
      How many shots: aim for a finished video of 65-85 seconds (hook ~4 s + the whole original + ~6 s per shot + outro ~5 s): clips of 25 s or more: 4-5 shots; 15-25 s: 5-6; under 15 s: 6-8 (use labels for clips with few cuts). Pick the most striking and most teachable moments, in time order, spread across the clip. Write numbers as digits; the voice reads them out.

Push nothing else: no other branches, no pull requests. Once main is pushed, nothing is left unpushed. GitHub's schedule then posts video 1 at 4:30 PM and video 2 at 9:30 PM Nepal time.
